## Life Update

Since I didn't get the chance to share it in last week's post, I went to watch the fireworks downtown after work on **July 31st**. The lack of audible backing music (there was only one place it was being broadcast from, and I wasn't sitting near it) made the experience far worse than previous years', even though the fireworks display itself was of comparable quality. Since the fireworks were funded by the city this year rather than by a corporate sponsor like previous times, this entire experience made me feel like I was watching my tax dollars being incinerated right in front of my eyes (which is technically accurate, but still...). At least I managed to catch an Early Music Vancouver ad at a bus stop on the way home. 

I had a day off from work on **August 1st**, so I went to Tsawwassen Mills to unwind. I was originally planning to go to an event somewhere else in Tsawwassen itself, but I ended up not going since it wasn't close to Tsawwassen Mills.

The **2nd** was a whirlwind of a day. It began with a routine resupply trip for the last performance of Orfeo, but shortly after I finished setting upat the Chan Centre, I needed to head out in the middle of my lunch and buy four bottles of prosecco at the nearest liquor store for post-opera cast celebrations. Thankfully, I was able to get there quite quickly on foot, and returned shortly before the show started with what I needed. However, it appears my purchase was rather unusual, as upon presenting the four bottles at checkout, the liquor store's cashier exclaimed: "Holy shit!"

My response? "It's for work."

Believe it or not, Early Music Vancouver's production of Monteverdi's Orfeo was my first-ever opera. Somehow, this music student never found an opportune time to watch one before, but I'm proud to say my first time was just as magical as I expected, especially since it was at (what is widely considered to be) the first proper opera.

Having a front-row seat close to the orchestra pit (and the sackbut section in particular), I was able to fully indulge in Moteverdi's innovative (for his time) use of harmonies, along with some colourful instrumentation. While I could wax lyrical about the music for hours on end, there were other aspects of the production that I really enjoyed as well.

The most obvious part was the cast's physicality on stage, especially with the way they expressed themselves through their body language, dancing, and other gestures no matter a scene's mood, and no one embodied this better than Marc Mauillon as Orfeo. Whenever he was on stage, I was entranced by the way he carried himself and interpreted the myriad emotions Orfeo feels with his actions throughout the opera, be it joy, sorrow, or hope.

Another aspect I liked were the simple yet striking costuming decisions, with the 'mortals' wearing simple tunics and pants, while the 'supernatural' characters wore more elaborate dresses and robes. Furthermore, the (possibly?) Renaissance-era paintings used as background visuals really left an impression on me, though I'll definitely need to ask around for their source later on.

Unfortunately, all good things must come to an end, and I had to immediately help with striking the stage after the opera was over. Once the instruments were packed away, I had to help transport them and other supplies between the Chan Centre, EMV's office, and Grace Vancouver Church for future rehearsals. This process took three hours in total, and I had to help move an organ, two harpsichords, and a whole host of refreshments and other stage supplies in the process.

In contrast, all I needed to do on the **3rd** was help set up Grace Vancouver Church for rehearsals, and got a rundown of the building's entry procedures for later on in the week.

The **4th** was the longest day I spent at work, since I first needed to be on standby during the rehearsal of Vivaldi's Four Seasons at Grace Vancouver Church, before walking over to the EMV office to mainly be on standby for another rehearsal. While I didn't hear the entirety of the Four Seasons during its rehearsal, I really enjoyed the movements they did, especially since the usage of historical instruments made the piece feel more primal and guttural. This made it sound akin to an actual force of nature, rather than the gentle elegance of most modern renditions.

At the office, I also helped answer a phone call and locked it up after a moving team came to pick up a fortepiano.

## Baked Pork Chop Rice Review

In this section, I'll be reviewing different restaurants' takes on my favourite dish - baked pork chop rice. For those of you who don't know what it is, baked pork chop rice is a Hong Kong dish comprised of a pork chop on top of rice, which is then covered in tomato sauce and cheese before being baked. The result is a gooey and juicy dish that's bound to leave you stuffed, and it's been my to-go meal at almost every place that serves it since I was young. While I've been keeping a mental scoreboard of every place I've had baked pork chop rice at, I thought putting it here would be a fun and unique section of blog, so expect more of it in the future.

My first review will be for the lobster sauce baked pork chop rice I ate at Tsawwassen Mills' Cha Don on the **1st**, which opened not too long ago. Even though they also serve regular baked pork chop rice, this was a variant I had never seen before, so I decided to try it out.

Sauce: 3/5 - The use of a creamy lobster-based sauce is certainly different than the usual tomato sauce, though at least it did taste like lobster. Unfortunately, there was a bit too much of it, so it made the rest of the dish feel like a soup at times.

Rice: 2/5 - Insufficiently fried, giving it a grainy yet mushy texture.

Pork chop: 3/5 - Merely pan-fried, though succulent and tender enough.

Additional toppings: 3/5 - Only mushrooms and bits of lobster, though both were rather tasty.

Portion size: 4/5 - Enough to last me for two meals, yet I wasn't feeling that stuffed for some reason.

Total score and final remarks: 15/25 - This dish fails to meet most of my regular standards for baked pork chop rice, especially in Vancouver, though I did get the lobster (the main reason I deviated from my usual order) I paid an extra $1 for. Assuming their regular baked pork chop rice is more consistent than this experimental dish, I'll likely be having it the next time I stop by.
