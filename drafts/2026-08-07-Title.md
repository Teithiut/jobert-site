## Baked Pork Chop Rice Review

In this section, I'll be reviewing different restaurants' takes on my favourite dish - baked pork chop rice. For those of you who don't know what it is, baked pork chop rice is a Hong Kong dish comprised of a pork chop on top of rice, which is then covered in tomato sauce and cheese before being baked. The result is a gooey and juicy dish that's bound to leave you stuffed, and it's been my to-go meal at almost every place that serves it since I was young. While I've been keeping a mental scoreboard of every place I've had baked pork chop rice at, I thought putting it here would be a fun and unique section of blog, so expect more of it in the future.

My first review will be for the lobster sauce baked pork chop rice I ate at Tsawwassen Mills' Cha Don on the **1st**, which opened not too long ago. Even though they also serve regular baked pork chop rice, this was a variant I had never seen before, so I decided to try it out.

Sauce: 3/5 - The use of a creamy lobster-based sauce is certainly different than the usual tomato sauce, though at least it did taste like lobster. Unfortunately, there was a bit too much of it, so it made the rest of the dish feel like a soup at times.

Rice: 2/5 - Insufficiently fried, giving it a grainy yet mushy texture.

Pork chop: 3/5 - Merely pan-fried, though succulent and tender enough.

Additional toppings: 3/5 - Only mushrooms and bits of lobster, though both were rather tasty.

Portion size: 4/5 - Enough to last me for two meals, yet I wasn't feeling that stuffed for some reason.

Total score and final remarks: 15/25 - This dish fails to meet most of my regular standards for baked pork chop rice, especially in Vancouver, though I did get the lobster (the main reason I deviated from my usual order) I paid an extra $1 for. Assuming their regular baked pork chop rice is more consistent than this experimental dish, I'll likely be having it the next time I stop by.
